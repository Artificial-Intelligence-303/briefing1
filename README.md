## What a briefing is

Three to five minutes, every member speaking, on what is actually happening in your group's area of
AI in the world. Not what AI might do one day. What it did, where, to whom, and how much.

**For this briefing you choose your own group.**

Later rounds come as their own repositories, and some of them will give you the reading rather than
asking you to find it. **This one asks you to find it.**

## The three things that make it a briefing rather than a summary

**1. Numbers.** Every development you report carries a figure with a unit and a date. Not "adoption
is growing" but "18 percent of firms reported using AI in a business function, up from 14 percent a
year earlier." If you cannot find a number, you have not found the source yet.

**2. Data you can point at.** The number comes from somewhere a reader can go and check: an agency
release, a filing, a survey, a dataset, a regulator's docket. Reporting about a number is fine, and
the report will tell you where the number came from. Follow it there.

**3. A visual.** One picture the class can read in ten seconds. **Make it or find it, either is
fine.** If you make it, say what data it is built from. If you find it, say who published it. Either
way the source goes in `briefing.md` and gets published under the image.

Good visuals for these areas look like: a chart of a quantity over time, a map of where something is,
a graph of who is connected to whom, or a comparison of two groups on the same measure. A screenshot
of a paragraph of text is not a visual.

## How to fill it in

1. Open `briefing.md` and read it end to end before you start writing.
2. Keep every `##` heading and every `- Label:` line exactly as they are. Write your answers after

4. Commit and push. Check that both files appear on GitHub.

Your briefing is published at **areweagentsyet.com/briefings/** and turned into a projection deck
automatically. **You do not need to make slides.** Present from the published deck.

## Using AI on this

Allowed, disclosed, and your responsibility. Full policy: **https://areweagentsyet.com/ai/**

The specific hazard here is worth stating plainly. Ask a chat model what happened recently in your
area and it will tell you fluently, in detail, with dates, and **with numbers**. Some of it will be
real. Some will be from two years ago described as though it were last month. Some will be a company,
a filing, or a statistic that does not exist. A fabricated number is harder to catch than a
fabricated story, because it looks like precision.

**Every development in your brief needs a link you personally opened, and every number needs to
appear on the page behind that link.**
